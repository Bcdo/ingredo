Ingredo

Mobile Design Spec · v1

A warm, kitchen-inspired companion for meal planning, recipes, and shared household shopping. Not a task manager — no checkboxes, no dashboards. Items are tapped off the list the way you'd pick them off a shelf, and land on a visible “Recently Purchased” shelf below. Cream linen surfaces, clay terracotta actions, sage confirmations. Fraunces for warmth, Karla for clarity.

Screens 01 + 02

Bottom navigation & Home

Navigation structure

Four tabs, all thumb-reachable one-handed: Today · Plan · Recipes · Shop. Four (not five) keeps targets ≥80pt wide. The household lives behind the avatar cluster in the header — it's social context, not a destination you visit hourly. The Shop tab carries a live badge with the active item count, so the answer to “do we need anything?” is visible without opening the list.

Home = “Today”, not a dashboard

  • Tonight's dinner is the hero — pulled straight from the meal plan. One tap opens the recipe ready to cook.
  • A shopping glance card shows the live item count and who added what, with a pulsing presence dot — collaboration you can feel.
  • Tomorrow peeks below tonight, nudging the planning habit without demanding it.
  • No stats, no charts, no streaks. A kitchen, not a cockpit.

Screen 03

Weekly meal plan

Rationale

A vertical week — one row per day — because that's how people think about dinners (“what's Wednesday?”), and it scrolls naturally with a thumb. Today is anchored with a clay date token so you always know where you are in the week.

  • Empty days are invitations, not blanks: a dashed “Add dinner” slot is itself the tap target. Tapping opens the recipe picker pre-filtered to favourites and recent.
  • Each meal card shows who planned it — a small avatar makes the plan feel co-owned.
  • Long-press a meal to drag it to another day; swipe left to remove. Both are mentioned in onboarding, neither is required.

The bridge to shopping

The sticky sage button — “Add week to shopping list” — is the app's keystone action. It aggregates every ingredient from planned meals, merges duplicates (2 onions + 1 onion = 3 onions), subtracts what's already on the list, and sorts by store category. Planning becomes shopping in one tap.

Screen 04

Recipe list

Rationale

A two-column visual grid — people choose dinner with their eyes. Cards are image-led with the title and time underneath; the heart marks household favourites (shared, not personal — “our” recipes).

  • Search sits at the top, always visible, searching titles and ingredients (“what can I make with halloumi?”).
  • Filter chips answer real weeknight questions: Quick (<30 min), Favourites, Vegetarian, Recently cooked. One tap, no filter screens.
  • The clay floating “＋” creates a recipe from anywhere in the grid — no hunting for an add button in a menu.

Flow

Tap a card → recipe detail. Long-press a card → quick actions sheet: Plan it · Add ingredients to list · Favourite — so frequent flows skip the detail screen entirely.

Screen 05

Recipe detail

Rationale

One scroll, three zones: hero → ingredients → steps. The sheet pulls up over the photo so content gets the space, not chrome.

  • The servings stepper and US ⇄ Metric toggle sit directly above the ingredients they transform. Tap “＋” and every quantity rescales in place — 250 g becomes 375 g before your eyes. No settings screen, no recalculating in your head at the stove.
  • Quantities are typeset in clay Fraunces — scannable at arm's length with wet hands.
  • “Add all to shopping list” respects the current serving count and merges into the household list by category. Individual ingredients can be excluded first with a tap (e.g. you already have olive oil).
  • Steps use generous 1.6 line-height and oversized numerals — readable propped against a salt jar.

Flow

Two stacked actions close the loop: Plan it (pick a day → lands in the week) and Add to shopping list. Both are reachable without scrolling past the steps.

Screen 06

Shopping list

The signature interaction — no checkboxes

Every item is a full-width tappable card. Tap anywhere on it and it squeezes, blooms sage green, and glides down to the “Recently Purchased” shelf below. There's nothing to aim at — the whole 60pt-tall card is the target, which is what one-handed, basket-in-the-other-arm shopping demands. The milk card shows the mid-tap state.

Built for the aisles

  • Items group by store category — Produce, Dairy, Bakery, Pantry — in walking order. The list empties top-to-bottom as you move through the store.
  • Quantities are pre-merged across recipes: the curry's onion and the bolognese's onion arrive as one card, “3 onions”, with the recipe origins listed beneath.
  • Items added by a partner carry their avatar and arrive live, with a pulsing presence dot in the header — you can see Maja is in the list right now.
  • The quick-add bar stays at the top: type “butter”, hit return, done. Ingredo auto-categorises it into Dairy.

Screen 07

Recently Purchased

A shelf, not a graveyard

Purchased items don't get struck through and dimmed into clutter — they move to a visible shelf styled like things you now own: dashed outlines, softened color, still fully legible. The metaphor is the kitchen counter after unpacking, not a completed to-do list.

Three jobs, one section

  • Undo: tapped the wrong thing in a busy aisle? One tap on the shelf item and it slides straight back to its category, quantities intact.
  • History: items group by time — this trip, earlier this week — answering “did we already buy parmesan?” without scrolling chat history with your partner.
  • Quick re-add: staples you buy weekly live here permanently. Milk runs out → open Shop → tap milk on the shelf → it's back on the list. Faster than typing, every single week.

A “Buy again” suggestion row surfaces the household's most frequently purchased items at the top of the shelf — Ingredo learns your staples without asking.

Screen 08

Add recipe

Rationale

Opens as a full-screen modal sheet — Cancel left, Save right, both always visible. Saving works at any point; a title alone is a valid recipe you can finish later (and offline).

  • The “Paste a link” strip comes first because most recipes start life on a website. One tap imports title, photo, ingredients, and steps; the form below becomes a review-and-fix pass instead of data entry.
  • Ingredients are typed as one natural line — “2 tbsp olive oil” — and Ingredo parses quantity, unit, and name into chips live. Parsed structure is what powers scaling, unit conversion, and list merging, but the user never fills three fields per ingredient.
  • Steps are plain numbered text boxes. Drag handles reorder both lists.
  • New recipes are household-shared by default — they appear on Maja's phone the moment they sync.

Screen 09

Empty states

Rationale

Empty screens are the app's first impression and its weekly reset — the shopping list empties after every trip, the plan empties every Sunday. So emptiness is treated as a calm, warm moment with exactly one obvious next action, never a dead end or a guilt trip.

  • Empty shopping list (shown): a tilted illustration tile, a friendly line, and two escape hatches — generate from this week's plan, or tap a “Buy again” staple. The fastest possible refill is one tap away.
  • Empty meal plan: “A fresh week. What are you hungry for?” with a Plan from favourites button that suggests three household favourites for instant placement.
  • Empty recipes (first run): leads with link-import — “Bring your first recipe” — because new users own zero recipes in Ingredo but dozens in browser tabs.

Screen 10

Offline states

Rationale — designed for the basement supermarket

Grocery stores are concrete boxes with no signal. Offline is therefore a first-class, expected condition, not an error: the entire UI keeps working — browse, cook, plan, add, and purchase — against the local store, and a sync queue replays everything when the connection returns.

  • The butter-yellow pill is informative, never alarming: no red, no exclamation marks, no blocked buttons. It says what's true — “your list is safe.”
  • Locally-made changes show a quiet “syncs later” chip with a slow spinner. Everything else looks and behaves exactly as online — taps still glide items to the shelf instantly (optimistic UI).
  • Partner avatars and live presence dots simply hide while offline rather than showing stale state.
  • On reconnect the pill flips to a brief sage “All caught up ✓” and fades. Conflicts resolve item-level, last-writer-wins, with purchases never lost.

Screen 11

Household sharing

Rationale

The household is ambient, not administrative. Day to day it shows up as avatars on meals and items, live presence dots, and changes appearing as they happen. This screen — reached by tapping the avatar cluster in any header — is where it's managed, rarely.

  • Joining must survive a kitchen conversation: a 6-letter code or a QR scan. No emails, no pending-invite purgatory — partner scans, and the shared plan appears on their phone.
  • One pool, no permissions. Recipes, plans, and lists belong to the household. Anyone can edit anything — matching how kitchens actually work and keeping the model simple enough to never think about.
  • The activity feed answers “what changed?” after a day apart — gentle, reverse-chronological, person-attributed. It's a glanceable answer, not a notification firehose; pushes are reserved for direct nudges (“list is ready for pickup”).
  • Household-level preferences live here once, for everyone: metric by default, week starts Monday.
